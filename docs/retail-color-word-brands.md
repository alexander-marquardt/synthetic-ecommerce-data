# Color-word brands and color-named lines (gen#312, decoy class 7)

Text search ranks a product first because its brand or line name holds a color word that does
not describe the product. #294 class 7 covers clothing; this batch carries the pattern into
food, drink, home, garden and tools (`prototypes/retail_drawings/color_word_brand_rows.py`).
Every structured attribute stays true: the SKU color, the wine style and the packaging
color are what the product is. Each title is five words or fewer and holds the brand or line.

Each row says what fixes its query: `generic` (a brand or color policy reads the brand or
color field) or `special: <policy>`. The rows are templates only: no derived style copies them.

## Invented brands

All six brands are invented; each was checked against well-known brands and chains, and the
test holds a deny list of near misses (for example Green Giant, Yellow Tail, Black & Decker,
Silver Spoon, Blue Diamond, Red Wing). Each draws a dedicated house style
(`color_word_brand_styles.py`) whose lockup text equals the brand field.

- Green Harrow, Yellow Skerry, Black Larch, Silver Sedge, Blue Heddon, Red Linnet.

The color-named lines are `Red Cask`, `Black Cask` and `Blue Cask` (one amber blended whisky
in three tiers, sold under an existing house brand), `Black Cherry` (clear sparkling water) and
`Belgian White` (an orange-gold wheat beer). The lines are invented names: the common retail
tier names for spirits are avoided on purpose.
