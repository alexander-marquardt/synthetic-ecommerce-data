# Drill bushings: a deep type from the ASME B94.33 jig bushing tables (gen#831)

`Tool Holding > Bushings > Drill Bushings` was a breadth row of two SKUs. It is now a modelled type, `product_types/cutting_tools/drill_bushings.py`, and its row is gone from `breadth_categories/`. `tests/test_type_drill_bushings.py` runs the per-type gates over it. It also runs checks of its own: every published ANSI number, body, length, head and bore is its table row's; a drill bushing's bore is a drill size inside its row's bore range; every type is built; and the breadth row's sizes are still built. The ER collet, the other half of gen#831, came first (gen#854).

**Source.** ASME B94.33 is sold, and no public copy was found, so the tables are makers'. The rows are in the standards library as `asme_b94_33_jig_bushings_in`. Each was copied by a script from the page's text layer or HTML table, not retyped, and the pages were read on 2026-11-05:

| Type | Source | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| P, headless press fit | Boneham Metal Products, *P Type ANSI Drill Bushings*, pp. 10-11 (<https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0585/4419/3741/files/P-Type.pdf>) | each body's bore ranges, finished outside diameter and lengths: 18 rows |
| H, head press fit | Boneham, *H Type*, pp. 15-16 (`H-Type.pdf`, same folder) | the P rows' bodies and bores with each head's diameter and thickness: 18 rows |
| SF, slip-fixed renewable | Boneham, *SF Type*, pp. 24-25 (`SF-Type.pdf`) | each body's bore ranges, outside diameter, ground under nominal to slip, and lengths: 15 rows |
| L, headless liner | AmesWeb, *Plain Type Liner Bushing (Type L)* (<https://amesweb.info/bushing/plain-type-liner-bushing-type-L.aspx>), citing ASME B94.33-1996 | each liner's inside and outside diameter and lengths: 8 rows |
| HL, head liner | Boneham, *HL Type*, p. 33 (`HL-Type.pdf`) | each liner's inside and outside diameter, head and overall lengths: 7 rows |

Boneham's L and SL pages are not published, so the L rows are AmesWeb's. Where the two sources share a liner body (5/16" to 2-1/4" inside), their diameters agree to the last place. AmesWeb's Type H page was not used. Its bodies after H-20 do not match its own Type P page: it lists 3/4" to 1" bores on four bodies from 2" to 2-1/4", for example. Three printed values are corrected or kept, and the table lists them under `misprints`. Boneham p. 16 prints the code `-14434` for H-144-34, and p. 24 prints `-32.34` for SF-32-34. Boneham p. 25 prints SF-88's finished outside diameter as 1.3750 to 1.3757, with the minimum above the maximum. That diameter is kept as printed, and only the nominal 1-3/8" is published.

B94.33 numbers a bushing by type, body in sixty-fourths and length in sixteenths: `P-48-8` is a headless press-fit bushing with a 3/4" body, 1/2" long. The bores are drill sizes: the number and letter drills of `asme_b94_11m_drill_series_in`, and the fractional sizes by sixty-fourths up to the largest bore the table lists (1-7/8", SF-144). That is 205 sizes, and a letter drill that equals a fractional size (E, 1/4") counts as the fractional size. The library's number drills start at #60, so the rows for #80 to #61 build only their 1/64" and 1/32" sizes. A line claims ASME B94.33, the standard the pages number their rows by, and its provenance names that table for every dimension.

**Axes.** A drill bushing line (P, H, SF) is one brand's bushings of one type, body, bore range and length, and its SKUs are a run of the drill sizes in that range. A liner guides no drill: its bore is the body of the renewable bushing it holds. So a liner line (L, HL) is one brand's liners of one type and length, and its SKUs are the bodies made in that length. There are 364 lines a brand can offer: 129 P, 123 H, 91 SF, 11 L and 10 HL. The types take turns, so every type comes round within five lines. Within a type, every row is offered once before any row is offered a second length. Per brand, the spec space is 6,338 drill bushings and 100 liners.

**Names.** `Type` is `Press Fit`, `Slip-Fixed Renewable` or `Liner`, and `Head Type` is `Head` or `Headless`. The census's `Bushing Head Type`, `Bushing Code` and `Drill Bushing Wall Type` are refused, because each repeats the leaf's own noun, as the ER collet's `Collet Series` was. The B94.33 number is published as `ANSI Number`, the name the roller chain gives its ANSI designation. The bore is `Size` (the breadth row's `size_attr` and the price key). For a drill bushing, `Drill Size` names the drill (`#30`, `F`, `1/8`) and `Decimal Equivalent` gives its diameter, as the jobber drill does. On a drill bushing, `Drill Size` leads with the leaf's own noun, so the name gate would refuse it. It is listed in `names.TYPE_SPECIFIC`, because the drill is a mating part, as a tap's tap drill is (census 12). The body is `Outside Dia.`, and the head is `Head Dia.` and `Head Height`. The length is `Length`, the length under any head, which is the breadth row's name and the screws' one. The exception is a head liner, which publishes `Overall Length`, because Boneham's HL code counts the head. The SF head is on a page that was not read (p. 35), so an SF record publishes no head, and its drawing proportions one from the H heads.

**Material.** The pages give hardened steel at 60-65 HRC, so a bushing is published as `Steel`, the breadth row's value. The hardness is not published.

**Brands.** The survey asked which brands of the cutting-tool pool make drill bushings. The pool is the breadth row's `cutting_tool` family, the same 15 brands that make the ER collet: Dravenholt, Hollisdane, Estrimore, Iron Heron, Kessingmoor, Tervasko, Quillon Carbide, Tessaly Cutters, Orrinford, Kaldermark, Pellanor, Wendlow Tool, Varrick Precision, Lark & Lathe and Crennick Works. Each is an invented tooling maker, and a drill bushing is sold in the toolholding section beside the drills it guides. The catalogue has no way to narrow a family's brands for one type, and no fact in the tables read calls for it, so the pool is wired unchanged. All 15 brands build drill bushings at `--lines 39000`, from Hollisdane's 166 SKUs to Lark & Lathe's 19. Quillon Carbide's name says carbide, and carbide drill bushings are made, but the tables read give steel only, so its bushings are steel too.

**Price.** The census has no bushings, so the model is `measured=False`: generated, not measured. A standard-tier press-fit bushing with a 1/2" bore is anchored at about 12 USD, inside the breadth row's 2 to 15 USD. A 0.45 log-log slope on the bore prices a 1-3/4" bore at about 1.8 times that and a #60 bore at about a third. The type multiplies the price (`TYPE_FACTORS`): 1.0 for press fit, 1.2 for a liner and 1.5 for a renewable bushing.

**Sizes.** A drill bushing line's run starts at the breadth row's sizes (1/8", 3/16", 1/4", 3/8" and 1/2") before any other bore. Each row of a type that holds one starts at a different one of the five, so the default build reaches them all.

**Share.** 0.016: 72 SKUs over 42 products at the default build, and 1,092 over 618 at `--lines 39000` (fill 618 of 624 lines, 99%).
